🔧 Three small fixes that make it exactly right

Not mistakes to be embarrassed about. Each one is a common trap, and fixing it deepens the idea.

1 · "The volume needs to be around 14"

If you already knew the answer was 14, you'd just jump straight there. 🤔

✓ Gradient descent never knows the answer. The measure only says "too loud, and roughly how much". The direction, not the destination.

2 · "2 spoons, then 1 spoon"

That halves the amount in one go, and it treats the spoons as the measure.

✓ The measure is how far off the taste is (the loss). The step is a small nudge: "a bit strong, a little less next time."

3 · "Too much chemical harms the crop; too little doesn't work"

A brilliant real-world example! It describes the goal, but not yet the method.

✓ Add the loop: spray a dose → check the pests and the crop → adjust a little → repeat. Finding the balance by repeated small corrections is gradient descent.

2 of 4 · embeddings

Meaning is a place 🗺️

3 of 4 · frozen

4 of 4 · prediction

It is predicting, not knowing 🎲

One token at a time, always: "what comes next?" — then it reads its own answer back and asks again.

The capital of Telangana is Hyderabad (91%) Secunderabad (4%) … (5%)

Nothing in there checks whether the answer is true. It checks whether it is likely.

Which is exactly why it is most dangerous when it sounds most confident — and why Act 4 exists.

Big picture · model vs harness

🧠 + 🧩 The Model Guesses the Next Token. The Harness Does the Rest.

What you call "ChatGPT" or "Claude" is two things working together: a model and the harness around it.

Inside the model: It's31°Candrainy→ ? guess one token → add it → guess again… until "stop"
🧠 The Model (inference)a brilliant autocomplete
  • Takes text in, predicts the next token
  • Repeats that very fast (dozens of tokens a second)
  • ✗ Can't browse, run code, or send email
  • ✗ Remembers nothing between calls (the harness resends the chat)
  • ✗ Doesn't decide what happens next
🧩 The Harness (agent program)ordinary software wrapped around the model
  • Packs the prompt: instructions + chat history + list of tools
  • Collects tokens into words, then reads the result
  • Decides: is this a final answer, or a request to use a tool?
  • Runs the tool (search, calculator, calendar) and feeds the result back
  • Loops until done, and adds memory, limits and safety checks
1 · You"What's the weather in Chennai?"
2 · HarnessAdds instructions + tool list, sends to model
3 · ModelWrites, token by token: get_weather("Chennai")
4 · HarnessSpots a tool call, runs it → 31°C, rain
5 · ModelReads the result, writes: "It's 31°C and rainy…"
6 · HarnessSees a final answer → shows it to you
💡 Myth: "ChatGPT searched the web." Reality: the model wrote a request to search; the harness did the searching.

Act 2 · two power moves

Move 1: show, don't describe 📋

Describing the style you want is hard. Showing two examples is easy — and works better.

Two examples and it has the pattern. The jargon for this is few-shot prompting — and you have been doing it since school.

Move 2: give it room to think 🧠

Add five words — "think step by step" — and hard questions get noticeably better answers. This sounds like superstition. It is not.

Act 3 · when it comes back wrong

Don't start over. Steer. 🎯

The most common mistake: a bad answer arrives, and people delete everything and rewrite the prompt from scratch.

The whole conversation is re-read every single time. So a correction is added information, not a fresh start:

  • "Too formal. Same content, how you'd explain it to a friend."
  • "The second point is wrong — students here don't pay that fee. Redo it without that."
  • "Good. Now cut it to half the length and keep the example."

Three rounds of steering beats one perfect prompt — and it is far less work.

Act 4 · the dials, and the edges

Two consequences you will feel every day

  • Creativity is a dial. Always pick the top word → dull and repetitive. Sometimes pick a lower one → surprising and creative. That dial has a name: temperature.
  • Hallucination is not a bug in the usual sense. The model optimises for plausible-sounding, not for true. A confident wrong citation is the system working exactly as built.

One of these you can turn. The other you have to guard against — and that is the rest of this act.

The dial · where is it?

🙈 ChatGPT and Claude have no dial. Or do they?

The dial is still there. It's just not yours to turn in the chat app.

1 · It's hidden, not gone

The company sets the temperature for you, inside the harness. You can't see the number or change it.

Why hide it? Most people would set it wrong, and one sensible default works for most chats.

Where the dial is visible:

🔗 Google AI Studio (free · slider on the right)
🔗 Claude Workbench (pick Haiku 4.5, then model settings)
🔗 OpenAI Playground (pick GPT-4.1)

⚠️ The newest models (Claude Sonnet 5, Opus 5, GPT-5) have removed the dial. The makers' advice: steer with your prompt instead. 👉 card 3

2 · Myth: "set your temperature to 0"

Typing that into ChatGPT does not touch the dial. The model only writes tokens; the dial lives in the harness, out of its reach.

It may still act calmer. That's not the dial moving. Your words changed the context, and the context changed the odds.

🔄 Regenerate / Retry = the same prompt, a fresh roll of the dice.

3 · What you can do: move the odds with words

Narrow the answer (like turning it down):

"Give the single most common…" · "Answer in one word" · "Use exactly this format:" · "Only use the text I pasted"

Widen the answer (like turning it up):

"Give 10 unusual…" · "Avoid the obvious ideas" · "Surprise me" · "Three very different options"

💡 The dial reshapes the odds you already have. Your prompt decides what the odds are in the first place. Next slide: proof.

The dial · your questions

🙋 "If the dial is disappearing, why learn it?"

Because the dice never went away

🎲 Every answer is still one roll from a set of odds. Only the knob was removed; the company now picks the setting.

🔄 It explains things you see every day: why the same prompt gives different answers, and why Regenerate works.

✅ It gives you a free check: ask twice. If the two answers disagree on a fact, the model wasn't sure. Verify it.

🛠️ Plenty of tools still have the knob: Google's Gemini, cheaper Claude and GPT models, and open models you can run yourself. You'll set it in code in Week 5.

When to turn it down, when to turn it up
🧊 Low
0 – 0.3		Facts, maths, code, extracting data, filling a fixed format, grading or classifying. Anything you want the same every time.
🙂 Middle
0.5 – 1		Everyday writing: emails, summaries, explanations. Sounds natural, not robotic.

⚠️ Low is not "more correct". It is more repeatable. A wrong answer at T = 0 is wrong every single time.

What you can do about it (with words)

🔁 Ask twice. Different answers = a close race = the model is guessing.   📄 Give it the source ("use only this text"), so the odds come from facts you supplied (Week 6).   🚪 Allow an exit: "If you are not sure, say so."   🔍 Verify anything that matters (all of Week 4).

Where these tools genuinely fail ⚠️

  • Anything after its cutoff. It does not know this month. It will not say so.
  • Exact recall. Citations, page numbers, quotes, links — plausible shapes, invented contents.
  • Counting and careful arithmetic. It is predicting digits, not calculating. Ask for the working, or use a calculator.
  • Your private world. Your college rules, your notes, your company's data. It has never seen any of it — that is Week 6.
  • Knowing that it does not know. The failure underneath all the others.
