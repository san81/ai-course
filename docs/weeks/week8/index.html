3 · Is it fair to everyone?

It learns the past, including its unfairness ⚖️

A real case, 2014–2018

A large company trained an AI to rank job applications using its past ten years of hiring. Most past hires were men, so it learned to mark down resumes that mentioned "women's" (like a women's chess club).

The company dropped the tool.

Closer to home

🎙️ Does an interview coach score "confidence" lower for an accent it heard less often?

🗣️ Does your app answer as well in Telugu as in English? (Week 5's break-it challenge!)

How would you test yours for fairness? A try it in two languages · B ask different people to try it · C both

4 · Is it honest? A story from 1966

The first chatbot, and the first warning 🗨️

MIT, 1966

Joseph Weizenbaum writes ELIZA: a small script that turns your words back into questions.

"I'm sad about my exams." → "Why do you say you are sad about your exams?"

No understanding at all. Not even a model: just rules.

Then

His own secretary, who had watched him build it, asks him to leave the room so she can talk to it in private.

He spends the rest of his life warning people about what he made.

"…extremely short exposures to a relatively simple computer program could induce powerful delusional thinking in quite normal people." — Joseph Weizenbaum, 1976

Checkpoint · the ELIZA effect

Why did she want privacy with a script? 🤔

The puzzle: she watched him write it. She knew it was only rules that turn your sentence into a question. It remembered nothing and understood nothing. Still, she asked her boss to leave the room.
AShe thought it truly understood herUnlikely: she saw it being built, line by line.
BIt felt like being listened to, with no judgementYes. It repeats your words, never judges, never gets bored. That feels like being heard.
CShe wanted to test whether it workedThen she'd test it in front of him. She wanted him out.
Type one letter, then send together

This is the ELIZA effect: we trust things that sound human, even when we know they aren't. Today's chatbots are far better at that feeling, so builders are responsible for what people believe about their tools.

4 · Is it honest?

Say it's AI. Say what it can't do 🔎

😶 Hides it

"Hi! I'm Priya from the admissions office. Yes, the deadline is 15 June."

No source. A person's name on a machine.

🔎 Open about it

"I'm an AI assistant. The 2026 brochure (p.4, checked 10 Oct) says 15 June. Please confirm on the college website before paying."

Which would you trust with your family's money? Type 1 or 2

ELIZA's lesson, 60 years later: when it sounds human, people trust it more than they should. So tell them.
