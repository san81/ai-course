Chroma: a database for meaning 🗄️

📦It stores every piece: its text, its 768 numbers, and its labels (week, slide).
🔎It finds the nearest pieces without checking every one, using an index. Like Maps not measuring every ATM in India.
💾It keeps them on disk, so you embed once, not every time Colab restarts. Free, open-source, runs inside Colab.

You measured why: 30× the tokens, 30× the cost, and details blur in a huge prompt.

Real libraries have millions of pages, and they change every day.

The verdict: what died is basic RAG, the version we built today. RAG became plumbing, inside almost every AI product, and grew upgrades:

Where RAG is going

Three more upgrades, three words to know 🧭

🤖Agentic retrieval

Search is a tool the model decides to use: search, read, "not enough", search again with better words.

📝 "Five parts of a brief, and what does temperature change?" It searches twice: once for each.

🌳Hierarchical parsing

Keep the document's shape: book → chapter → section → page, like a table of contents. Not random 600-character cuts.

📝 Ours already has a tiny one: week → slide → title. That's why every answer can say [W3 · slide 24].

🎒Context engineering

Deciding everything that goes into the prompt, and what stays out: instructions, the right pieces, the date, earlier messages, tool results.

📝 Like packing for an exam: everything you need, nothing that weighs you down.

RAG or fine-tuning?

Two ways to "teach" a model ⚖️

📖 RAG adds facts

The model reads your documents at the moment you ask.

Update the documents → the answers update. No training.

🎓 Fine-tuning changes behaviour

More training, on your examples: a style, a format, a narrow skill.

Costs time and data. A poor way to teach facts that change.

🖥️ Live · 2 minutesTeachable Machine: train a model on ✋ vs ✌️ from my webcam. That's training, in your browser.
🏭 For language modelsHosted fine-tuning exists (upload examples, pay for training). Most capstones won't need it.
