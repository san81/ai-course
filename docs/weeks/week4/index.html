💬 Your three questions, answered

"Why do ChatGPT and Google AI Studio give different answers to the same question?"

All three of your guesses are right:

🧠 Different models, trained on different data → different odds for the next word

📋 Different hidden instructions and tools (web search on or off)

🎲 Different settings, like temperature

Like two good doctors from different colleges: both reasonable, not identical.

"Same tool, different answer every time. Which one is best?"

Why: at every word the model gives odds, and the app rolls the dice. One different pick early on, and the rest of the answer takes a different road. 🛣️

🎲 Same temperature = same dice, not the same roll. Every run is a fresh throw.

🎯 The dice only matter when the odds are close. Sure → same answer. Unsure → it changes.

✍️ Wording, ideas: pick the one that fits you. Variety is a feature.

📌 Facts, numbers: if runs disagree, the model was guessing. Check a real source.

Who is in control? 🧠 🤖

✅ You control the AI

Your head, its hands. You decide what to ask, check what comes back, and choose what to keep. AI does the heavy lifting: drafting, searching, summarising.

You can explain every line of your work.

⚠️ The AI controls you

Its head, your hands. It decides the answer; you just copy, paste and submit. You accept what it says without checking.

Ask you a question about "your" work, and you're stuck.

Same tool, same prompt box. The only difference is who is doing the thinking. Lend it your hands, never your head.

16 January 1913

A letter arrives in Cambridge ✉️

A 25-year-old clerk at the Madras Port Trust, born in Erode, raised in Kumbakonam, writes to a famous mathematician: G. H. Hardy.

Nine pages. Dozens of results. Formulas nobody had ever seen.

No proofs. Just the answers.

What Hardy did

Brilliant? Or a fraud? 🤔

  • First instinct: suspicion. Some formulas "seemed scarcely possible to believe."
  • So he checked. He and his colleague Littlewood worked through them, one by one.
  • Many were astonishing and new. Hardy: they "must be true, because, if they were not true, no one would have the imagination to invent them."
  • And some were wrong. Others had already been discovered by someone else.

Hardy invited him to Cambridge. Together they did some of the finest mathematics of the century: intuition, plus checking.

The lesson

Your new habit

Check any citation in 60 seconds ⏱️

1 · Does it exist?Paste the exact title in quotes into Google Scholar. No hit = stop, it's probably invented.
2 · Right people, place, time?Do the authors work in that field? Did the journal exist that year? Does the year fit?
3 · Does it say that?Open it. Use Ctrl + F to find the claim. A real paper that doesn't support the sentence is still a wrong citation.
4 · Can't open it?Then you can't cite it. Read the abstract at least, or find a source you can read.

Rule for this course: never paste a citation from a chatbot into your work until it has passed steps 1 and 3.

Under the hood

How does search actually find things? 🔎

1 · IndexLike the index at the back of a textbook: every word, and which pages it appears on. Built before you ask.
2 · MatchFind pages that contain your words, or, in newer systems, your meaning (remember embeddings?).
3 · RankDecide which ten you see first, out of millions. That ranking is where the magic, and the bias, lives.

This field of computer science is called information retrieval. Deep Research is a model that retrieves, reads, and retrieves again. NotebookLM retrieves from your files. You'll build one in Week 6.

description: Use when I paste citations or
  a reference list and ask if they are real.

For every citation:
1. Search the exact title. Not found → ❌
2. Do the authors, journal and year fit?
3. Open it, find the claim. Close but
   not exact → ⚠️
4. Can't open it → say "can't verify".
   Never guess.

Act 3 · using it honestly

A traffic light for AI in your studies 🚦

🟢 Go

Explain a concept, quiz me, give feedback on my draft, brainstorm, fix grammar, help me plan.

AI as a tutor. You still do the thinking.

🟡 Disclose

Restructure or rewrite parts of my work, summarise readings I will cite, suggest sources I then verify and read.

Fine if allowed, and you say so.

🔴 Stop

Submit AI text as my own, cite sources I never opened, use it where it's banned (a closed-book test).

That's not help. It's someone else's work with your name on it.

Your university's or teacher's rule always wins over this slide. When unsure: ask, and disclose.

You decide 🚦

One test to remember

The viva test 🎤

Before you submit anything, ask yourself:

"Could I explain and defend every sentence of this, out loud, without the AI?"

Yes → it's your work, however you got there.
No → you've outsourced the part where you learn. That's the real cost, not getting caught.

🤖 How Jev helps

Jev is a small decision model: it doesn't write paragraphs, it picks.

"Which board answer does this guess mean… or none of these?" → answer + how sure

✅ ≥ 75% score it · 🤔 45–75% "Did you mean…?" · ❌ below: not on the board

🎯 Trained with RLCD, Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions: when it says 90%, it's right about 90% of the time.
